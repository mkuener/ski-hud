What to measure

  1. OLED board (calipers): PCB width × height, PCB thickness, glass thickness above the PCB, the gap from the pin edge to the glass, the tallest part on the back. If the header is fitted: glass front to pin tips.
  2. Frame rail at the top centre: thickness from the top surface to the inside surface (railT), and its depth front-to-back (railD).
  3. Top vent windows ✓ measured: centre window 26.13 × 10.19 mm, ribs 4 mm. Still useful: the window's front-to-back width at its middle (the windows have a step), for stemD. Cut a slit in the vent foam strip under the centre window for the stem.
  4. Lens seat: the bar must stay behind the rail's front edge where the lens sits (barZ).
  5. Lens gap: from the vent centre forward to the inside of the lens, about 3–5 mm below the rail (lensGap). The fit check needs the pod to stay 1 mm short of it.
  6. Face foam thickness behind the rail (foamD, for the bench clip only).
  7. Cable outer diameter (cableD). Default 4.6 mm = variant A's coil, so the pack boot is the same.
  8. Strap width and thickness (pack clamp plate).

Design

Bench fixture (print first, tomorrow morning)

  • Clip slides onto the goggle's top edge from behind, jaws over the frame rail, spine behind the face foam. A 0.5 mm bump grips; no goggle measurement needed beyond railT and foamD.
  • Cradle holds the OLED upside down (pins at the bottom) so the Dupont plugs point back and down, clear of the frame. Slide the board in from the top between the grooves; it sits on the bottom bar.
  • Hinge: 34 mm of 1.75 mm filament through the ears and the clip's cheeks. The cheeks are 0.1 mm wider than the gap: it holds any angle by friction. Find the best angle, read it with a phone angle app, and put it in tilt for the final pod.
  • The board is upside down, and the lens mirrors the image: flip it in firmware (SSD1306 segment remap 0xA0/0xA1 and COM scan 0xC0/0xC8).

Screen pod + stem

  • Body holds the board face to the lens behind a 22.7 × 11.9 mm window over the active area (45° chamfer). The PCB front rests on four corner pads, clear of the glass and the panel flex; it locates on its outline (0.2 mm clearance).
  • Light hood: brow over the top (stops vent light), straight cheeks, chin flared 45° (stops your eye seeing the panel from below, never cuts the rays going down to the lens).
  • Lid clicks into the back with two side tabs; four corner posts press the PCB onto the pads. Push a pin into the side windows to open it.
  • Stem on the body goes up through the vent and clicks into the bar floor. Pod and bar clamp the rail: the bearing pads sit on the ribs either side of the window. The 4 wires run up the channel between the stem's arms. A clear 0.5 mm sheet sits in a pocket behind the window.
  • For the goggle pod the pin header has to go: cut the plastic carrier off, snip the pins to about 1 mm, solder the 4 wires to the stubs from behind.

Frame bar + 4-wire route

  • One 4-core cable from the pack (VDD 3.3 V, GND, SCK, SDA) enters through the TPU boot at the +X end. Inside, a cable clamp (saddle in the base, post in the lid, two 0.3 mm ribs) grips the jacket: strain relief, a pull never reaches a solder joint.
  • The wires go to the IMU's pads (VIN GND SCL SDA); from the same pads 4 short wires go back along the floor, down the stem channel, into the pod's top hole and to the OLED pads. Both chips share the I2C bus (OLED 0x3C, ICM-20948 0x68/0x69).
  • A drop of hot glue or silicone in the pod's wire hole stops the wires rubbing.

Strap pack

  • Same as variant A (ESP32 Lite + 103450 LiPo), copied here with the cable diameter as a parameter. The internal service connector becomes 4-pin (JST 1.25). With the default 4.6 mm cable the STLs match variant A's pack.

Assembly order

  1. Bench test: slide the OLED into the cradle (glass toward the front lips, pins at the bottom). Push the filament pin through ear, cheek, cheek, ear (clip cheeks between the ears). Plug 4 Dupont jumpers: VDD→3V3, GND→GND, SCK→GPIO22, SDA→GPIO21 (check with the firmware). Push the clip onto the goggle's top edge from behind, look through the goggles, set the angle.
  2. Goggle pod: remove the header, solder 4 wires (about 60 mm, 26–28 AWG silicone) to the pads from behind, thread them out through the body's top hole and up the stem channel. Board into the body, lid clicks on.
  3. From inside the goggle push the stem up through the vent and the bar base's floor slot until it clicks.
  4. Cable through the bar boot and over the clamp saddle; solder cable and pod wires to the IMU pads; IMU onto its pads; lid on (it squeezes the clamp).
  5. Pack: as variant A, with a 4-pin JST 1.25 pair as the internal disconnect.
