Your idea

It works, and it's REKKIE's layout. REKKIE's main board has one 10-pin socket for a coiled cable to the goggle; yours is the same with a pigtail instead of a board-mounted socket.

  • socket_board: 8-pin female JST 1.25 pigtail, wires soldered straight into the ESP32 Lite's holes. No header pins needed, board stays flat.
  • socket_battery: the ESP32 Lite's own 2-pin socket. The battery plugs straight in once we know the type (looks like PH 2.0).
  • loop_plug_board: 8-pin male pigtail spliced to the coiled cable.
  • loop cable: 8-core coiled cable, ~16 cm relaxed.
  • goggle end: cores soldered straight to the screen and IMU (recommended), or a second plug pair (your version, switch above).

Changes I'd make

  • No coax. REKKIE's coil is ordinary thin wires; the screen runs fine over plain wire at this length. Coax would be thick, stiff and hard to solder.
  • 8 wires. Screen and IMU together would need 12, but 12-core coiled cable is rare (CHF 38 at the one seller). Two bridges on the screen remove two wires: CS to GND (it's the only device on its bus) and RES to VCC (the firmware resets it with a command). The IMU takes power from the screen's pins. 8-core coil costs about CHF 5.
  • One plug, at the pack. That's where you unplug (charging, swapping lenses, taking the pack off). A second plug in the goggle adds 8 more splices and a 13 mm housing in the tightest spot. The goggle end gets soldered directly, as REKKIE's is.
  • Strain relief. Solder joints crack if the wire flexes at the joint. Every pigtail and the coil get clamped or hot-glued 5–10 mm from the joint, in the housing.
  • Heat-shrink every splice and keep splices staggered so the bundle stays thin.
  • Check battery polarity with a multimeter before the first plug-in.
