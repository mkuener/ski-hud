Why the hand-held test looked wrong

  1. Small. Without a lens you can't bring a 10 mm panel close enough to look big and still focus. With the doublet it fills about 15.5° × 11.7°, like a 1 m wide screen 4 m away.
  2. Blurry. An eye can't focus at 2–3 cm. Put the panel 22 mm behind the doublet's flat face and the lens sends it out to a few metres.
  3. Doubled. The goggle lens is two lenses with four surfaces, and each one reflected its own copy. A separate flat combiner fixes this: the film reflects much more than the plastic behind it, and light from the lens is parallel, so reflections from the combiner's two flat faces land in the same place.
  4. Too close. Same cause as blurry: the picture sat 2 cm from your eye. After the lens it sits about 4 m away.

Change from HARDWARE.md: the film goes on its own small tilted piece of clear plastic, not on the goggle lens.

The goggle lens is curved around your head. Any patch of it can only reflect light from close to your own eye back into your eye, so there's nowhere to put the projector. A combiner tilted at about 45° solves that. It also keeps the HUD light out of the Cat 4 lens, which would let only 6.4 % through.

Build order

  1. Bench check, no cutting. Run the HUD and hold the doublet with its flat face 22 mm from the panel. Look into the curved face from 5 cm. The text should be big and sharp, and should stay sharp when you glance past it at the far wall.
  2. Mock-up pod from foam board or card using the build sheet: doublet curved face down, mirror 11 mm above the flat face at 45°, panel facing the mirror.
  3. Combiner. Cut a 26 × 18 mm piece of 1 mm clear plastic (a CD case works) and stick the film on the side that faces your eye.
  4. Hold the mock-up over the goggles with the combiner in front of your right eye and check the eye view. Only then cut the slot in the frame top.
  5. Focus. Look at something far away and slide the panel away from the lens until the text starts to soften. Then come back about 0.3 mm. That puts the image at about 4 m.
  6. Final pod: 3D-print it from the same dimensions and glue it to the frame top.
