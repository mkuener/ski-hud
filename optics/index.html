Why the hand-held test looked wrong

  1. Small. Without a lens you can't bring a 10 mm panel close enough to look big and still focus. With an f30 doublet it fills about 15.5° × 11.7°, like a 1 m wide screen 4 m away.
  2. Blurry. An eye can't focus at 2–3 cm. Put the panel about 27 mm behind the doublet's flat face and the lens sends it out to a few metres.
  3. Doubled. The goggle lens is two lenses with four surfaces, and each one reflected its own copy. A separate flat combiner fixes this: the film reflects much more than the plastic behind it, and light from the lens is parallel, so reflections from the combiner's two flat faces land in the same place.
  4. Too close. Same cause as blurry: the picture sat 2 cm from your eye. After the lens it sits about 4 m away.

Changes from HARDWARE.md

The film goes on its own small tilted piece of clear plastic, not on the goggle lens. The goggle lens curves around your head, so any patch of it can only reflect light from close to your own eye back into your eye. A combiner tilted at about 45° solves that, and keeps the HUD light out of the Cat 4 lens, which would let only 6.4 % through.

Like REKKIE, the optics sit inside the frame. The space above your eye is only 20–25 mm deep, so the Ø25.4 mm doublet can't fit. It's swapped for a Ø12.7 mm f30 doublet. With the shorter light path inside the goggle, the small lens gives a bigger eyebox than the big lens did on top.

REKKIE skips the lens: a bare display reflects off a coated patch on the goggle lens, which acts as a weak curved mirror. Traced with this panel, that puts the image only 7–12 cm from your eye, slightly smeared by the lens curve, and you have to refocus away from the slope to read it. The small doublet puts the image about 4 m out instead, sharp while you look down the slope.

Build order

  1. Order the small lens: a Ø12.7 mm, f = 30 mm achromatic doublet (Thorlabs AC127-030-A, or a clone sold as "12.7 mm achromatic lens f30").
  2. Bench check, no cutting. Run the HUD. Hold the doublet with its flat face about 27 mm from the panel and look into the curved face from 2–3 cm. The text should be big and sharp, and should stay sharp when you glance past it at the far wall.
  3. Cut the mirror to 15 × 15 mm with a glass cutter. Score the plain glass side, never the silvered side.
  4. Combiner. Cut a 16 × 16 mm piece of 1 mm clear plastic (a CD case works) and stick the film on the side that faces your eye.
  5. Card mock-up of the module using the build sheet: doublet curved face down, mirror 7.5 mm above its flat face at 45°, panel facing the mirror from the nose side. Hold it inside the goggles while wearing them and check where the HUD appears.
  6. Focus. Look at something far away and slide the panel away from the lens until the text starts to soften. Then come back about 0.25 mm. That puts the image at about 4 m.
  7. Final module: 3D-print it with a clip that follows the frame's top edge, like REKKIE's. The driver board sits against the inside of the frame between your eyes. The ESP32 and LiPo go in a pack on the strap, with a cable that runs under the frame foam.
