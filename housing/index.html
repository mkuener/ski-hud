Design

Strap pack

  • Shape: R3.5 corners, R2 edges on the head side, R3 on the outer face. The inside follows the outside, so every wall is 1.6 mm (top 1.8). A 0.4 mm shadow line at the seam hides any step between lid and base.
  • Stack: battery on the head side in a rib cradle (0.3 mm room for the pouch to swell), ESP32 Lite on foam tape above it, button under the lid. The 8-pin connector pair and the coil splices sit in a channel beside the battery.
  • Battery plugs into the board's own socket. The socket sits beside USB-C, so the pack is plug room longer at that end; the wires get a notch in the lid's lip.
  • Lid: a 3.5 mm lip runs all the way round inside the base (a labyrinth: water has to climb up and over it). Four tabs click into blind pockets, so the wall has no holes. The bumps have a 45° back face: firm, but a flat screwdriver in the pry slot at either end opens it without breaking anything.
  • Button: TPU cap with a thumb dish. Its flange sits in a recess under the lid and the switch spring presses it up against the lid, which seals the hole. The switch snaps into a pocket with two fingers, so pressing hard can't push it in.
  • USB-C is the only opening: a 13.2 × 7.0 mm tunnel with a chamfered mouth. The TPU plug seals it with two ribs and hangs on a tether so it can't get lost.
  • Coil: a TPU boot sits in the front wall (flange inside, shoulder in a countersink) and tapers over 12 mm, so the cable bends gradually instead of kinking at the wall.
  • Water: splash- and snow-proof, not waterproof. Three 1.5 mm weep holes in the wall that faces down when worn let meltwater and condensation out and let the air pressure even out.
  • Strap clamp: the plate snaps under the base with four hooks and squeezes the strap in a ribbed channel. It touches your head, so all its edges are fully rounded.

Screen pod + frame bar

  • Goggle (COPOZZ, magnetic lens): the top rail is a ladder of vent windows with 4 mm ribs; a 26.13 × 10.19 mm window sits at the top centre (measured). The HUD clamps the two ribs at that window's ends; the magnetic lens sits on the frame's front face and stays free to come off and snap back on.
  • Window clamp: two legs on the pod: each has a pad that pushes up under one of the centre window's end ribs and a spring arm that rises through the window just inside that rib. The bar's floor sits on top of the same ribs and the catches hook outward over it, so both ribs are clamped top and bottom and the arms locate on the window's ends; knocks go into the frame, not the snaps. The arms flex inward, into the open window, and the wires run up between them.
  • Pod hangs inside the goggle between lens and face foam, screen facing the lens, tilted down; the lens's dark glossy inner face is the mirror. The window covers the glass edge by 1 mm and has a 45° bezel. A hood (brow + side cheeks, hood slider) keeps light from the vents and the sides off the screen. Fit checks warn if it gets too close to the lens, your face or your line of sight, and suggest a tilt from your eye height.
  • Bar sits on the rail centred over the window, set back so its front (even at its ends, where the frame curves back) stays behind the lens seat. It is 32 mm long, with the IMU centred on four 1.8 mm pads so it clears the catches, clamped by four posts in the lid. Wires go down to the pod through a hole in the middle of the floor. The coil's TPU boot lies in a U-notch and the lid closes it. Same snaps, shadow line and pry slot as the pack.
  • Vent foam: if foam covers the centre window from below, cut two small slits in it for the arms (and one for the wires) next to the window's ends.

Making it

  • Materials: PETG for every hard part (black or dark grey; the pod must be opaque). TPU 95A for the button cap, port plug and the two coil boots.
  • Printer: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 4 walls for PETG, 100 % infill for the TPU parts. The orientation is in the parts table and the STL files already lie that way. Nothing needs supports; give the pod back and the boots a brim.
  • Clearances: 0.15 mm per side on sliding fits (lids, lips), 0.3 mm around the battery and the port plug body, 0–0.05 mm where TPU meets PETG (TPU squeezes). Snap bumps reach 0.25–0.35 mm into the other part.
  • Snap strain stays at about 2.5 % or less (2.8 % in the worst slider case; PETG gives way at about 4 %), so every snap survives being opened again.
  • Smallest features: 0.8 mm (switch fingers), text strokes 0.5–0.6 mm, 0.3–0.4 mm deep. Every face that sits on the bed starts on a round, so a squashed first layer doesn't change any fit.
  • Test first: print the bar lid and base (5 g) to check your printer's fit before the big pack parts.
  • Ready-made files: site-src/housing/stl/ holds all 11 STLs at the current defaults (closed meshes, print orientation), with a README. Safe to print now: pack base, pack lid, button cap, port plug, both boots, bar base, bar lid (window and ribs are measured; rail thickness only moves the bar). Wait: strap plate (strap width/thickness), pod back (rail thickness, tilt, pod gap), and the pod front until the lens, face and view checks pass with your lens-to-foam depth and eye height. Set the sliders and use the STL buttons above, or re-export with node site-src/housing/export-stl.mjs.

Assembly order

  1. Slide the pack's TPU boot onto the coil, narrow end toward the goggle. Push the coil through the pack's front hole from outside, then press the boot in, flange first, until the flange pops behind the wall. Splice the coil to the 8-pin male pigtail (heat-shrink).
  2. Solder the female pigtail, button wires (GPIO27, G), the matching plug on the battery leads (check polarity against the board's "+" with a multimeter before plugging in) and the 100 kΩ/100 kΩ divider (battery + → GPIO35 → G, lying flat on top of the board near the left pin row) to the ESP32 Lite.
  3. Pack base: battery into the rib cradle on foam tape, Kapton over it, board on 2 mm foam tape, connector pair and splices into the channel, coil into the cradle with a drop of glue.
  4. Lid, upside down: TPU cap into the hole from inside, then press the switch into its pocket until both fingers click (legs trimmed to 1.5 mm). Snap the lid on: four clicks.
  5. Port plug: push the tether's mushroom pin into the small hole under the USB-C tunnel (flat pliers help), then press the plug into the tunnel.
  6. Goggle end: thread the coil's other end through the bar's boot and lay the boot in the bar's U-notch; solder 6 wires down through the bar's centre wire hole and the centre window to the screen; bridge CS→GND and RES→VCC on the screen; IMU to SDA/SCL + power.
  7. Screen into the pod front, pod back clicks on. Lens off. Set the bar on the rail centred over the middle window. From inside the goggle push the pod's two arms up through that window, just inside its end ribs, and through the bar's floor until both catches click. Feed the wires up between the arms through the bar's centre hole; a dab of silicone on the hole and round the arm slots keeps the goggle's damp air away from the IMU. IMU onto its pads, lid on, lens back on.
  8. Strap plate: lay the strap in the channel, snap the plate under the pack.
  9. To open: pack and bar lids lift with a small flat screwdriver in the pry slot at the seam. The pod comes off when you lift the IMU off its pads (bar lid off), pinch the two catch tips together with tweezers and pull the pod down. The pod front releases by pressing its two bumps through the side windows with a pin.
